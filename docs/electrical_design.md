# Electrical design (purchased hardware)

Status: **design + calculations** for the parts the team bought. Nothing here has been measured on
the built robot yet. Items marked **MEASURE** must be checked on the bench before the first powered
run (see `calibration_guide.md`). The full drawing is `docs/Circuit_Diagram.pdf` (page 1 schematic,
page 2 wire-by-wire list, page 3 charging and safety), generated by `tools/make_wiring_diagram.py`.

The original design (N20 + encoders, QTR-MD-08A, TB6612, 2S LiPo) is kept in the simulator as the
`n20_hardware()` preset and in the CAD; this page describes the robot actually being built.

## 1. Power tree
```
 4 × 18650 in series (4S): 16.8 V full, 14.8 V nominal, stop at ~12.8 V
   │
   ├─ 3 A glass fuse (twist holder) ── KCD1 rocker switch ──► VBAT (fused, switched)
   │
   VBAT ──┬─► L298N "12V" terminal (VS)        OUT1/OUT2 ─► left BO motor
   │      │      (+ 470 µF 35 V recommended)   OUT3/OUT4 ─► right BO motor
   │      │      5V-EN jumper REMOVED
   │      └─► LM2596 IN+  ── set to 5.0 V ──► ESP32 VIN  and  L298N "5V" terminal (logic)
   │                                              └─ ESP32 AMS1117 ─► 3V3 rail
   │                                                    ├─ ESP32-WROOM-32 (radios never started)
   │                                                    ├─ 5-ch IR array VCC
   │                                                    ├─ 2 × FC-51 VCC
   │                                                    └─ MPU6050 VCC
   └─ (optional) VBAT ─ 47 kΩ ─ GPIO26 ─ 10 kΩ ─ GND     battery sense, MESH_VBAT_SENSE=1
```
Every ground (battery −, LM2596 IN−/OUT−, L298N GND, ESP32 GND, all sensors, LED) is one common
ground. Highest potential difference on the robot: **16.8 V**, below the 24 V limit (rule, p.5).

## 2. Component checks
| Check | Value | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Pack voltage vs. parts | 16.8 V full | L298N VS ≤ 46 V ✔; LM2596 Vin ≤ 40 V ✔; L298N's own 78M05 is only for ≤ 12 V input, so **its 5V-EN jumper is removed** and the logic gets 5 V from the LM2596 ✔ |
| Motor rating vs. pack | BO motors are a 6 V class part (**MEASURE** the label/rating) | Firmware clamps the duty so the **average** motor voltage is ≤ `motor_max_avg_v` = 6.0 V (`duty ≤ 6.0 / Vbat`). Without battery sensing Vbat is assumed to be `vbat_nominal_v` = 16.2 V (full pack), so a fresh pack gives the motors about 6 V and a half-empty pack less, never much more. If your motors are rated 12 V, raise `motor_max_avg_v`. |
| L298N current | BO stall ~1 A at 6 V (estimate; **MEASURE** with a bench supply, < 1 s) | L298N 2 A per channel ✔. The L298N drops ~2 V (bipolar transistors); feed-forward calibration absorbs it. |
| Stall / jam | no encoders, so a blocked wheel is not seen directly | Stall check: more than `stall_no_event_mm` = 3200 mm of modelled travel with no junction, corner or dead end ⇒ STALL fault, motors off (the arena is 2.3 m, every line ends in a feature). Simulated detection ≈ 7 s at exploration speed. |
| 3V3 rail load | ≈ 135 mA typical, 235 mA worst (`hardware/power_budget.md`) | AMS1117 (1 A): 0.4 W ✔. **MEASURE** the regulator part number on your board. |
| 5 V buck | ≈ 1.4 W out | LM2596 (3 A) ✔ |
| Peak battery current | ≈ 2 A for short moments | 18650 cells ✔; 3 A fuse ✔ |
| ESP32 input pins | all sensors powered from **3V3** | IR array, FC-51 and MPU6050 outputs stay ≤ 3.3 V. ESP32 GPIOs are **not** 5 V tolerant: never power the sensors from 5 V. |

## 3. Signal compatibility (all 3.3 V)
| Signal | Source | ESP32 side | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| IR array D1-D5 | LM339 comparator, open collector with pull-up to the module VCC (3.3 V) | ADC1 pins 36, 39, 34, 35, 32 | Read through the ADC: a digital output reads ~0 or ~4095. Each channel's polarity is learned during the calibration sweep (`sensor_auto_polarity`). |
| FC-51 OUT | comparator, pulled up to 3.3 V | GPIO33 (ADC1), GPIO25 (ADC2) | ADC2 is fine because Wi-Fi is never started. LOW = reflection (white). |
| MPU6050 SDA/SCL | GY-521 has on-board pull-ups | GPIO21 / GPIO22, 400 kHz | Gyro Z read every 1 ms; bias estimated whenever the robot stands still. If the MPU stops answering 20 times in a row the firmware falls back to the speed model for heading. |
| L298N ENA/ENB, IN1-IN4 | ESP32 GPIO (3.3 V) | — | L298 V<sub>IH</sub> = 2.3 V ✔. PWM at 1 kHz on ENA/ENB (`esp32_l298n` build): the L298N switches slowly, higher frequencies waste power in it. |
| Battery sense (optional) | 47 kΩ / 10 kΩ divider | GPIO26, 2.95 V at 16.8 V | inside the calibrated ADC range (`analogReadMilliVolts`) |
| START | GPIO0 BOOT button (board pull-up) | — | Do not hold it while powering on (that enters download mode). |
| Red LED | GPIO15 | weakest pin drive (`GPIO_DRIVE_CAP_0`) | A 330 Ω series resistor is still recommended. |

## 4. Noise and grounding
* Battery − goes to the L298N GND terminal; the LM2596 and ESP32 grounds meet there too. Motor
  current then does not flow through the sensor ground wires.
* Recommended: 470 µF 35 V across the L298N 12V-GND terminals, 100 nF ceramic across each motor.
  Twist each motor pair. Keep sensor wires away from motor leads.
* The MPU6050 is sensitive to vibration: mount it flat near the axle centre, on foam tape.

## 5. Battery and charging (important)
* The **TP4056 charges one cell (4.2 V)**. It cannot charge the 4S pack and must never be connected
  to it. Two ways to charge:
  * **Recommended:** a 4S BMS board plus a 16.8 V Li-ion charger (≈ ₹600 total, estimate). The BMS
    also cuts off on over-discharge, which matters while the robot cannot measure its battery.
  * **With what you have:** take the cells out and charge each one in a single holder on the
    TP4056 until its LED changes colour. All four cells then start equal.
* Charge fully before every match. Stop using the pack below ~12.8 V (3.2 V/cell): check with a
  multimeter between runs.
* Insert cells with the right polarity; strap the holder to the chassis (nothing may fall on the arena).
* Radios: the firmware never starts Wi-Fi or Bluetooth and calls `btStop()` at boot (rule p.6).

## 6. Battery voltage without sensing
The no-encoder speed model converts a wanted wheel speed into a motor voltage, then into a duty using
the battery voltage. Without the divider the firmware assumes `vbat_nominal_v` = 16.2 V. Simulation
(sample arena, harsh profile, 4 seeds, measured before the motor-trim learning was added): with the firmware's battery voltage 10 % off and a 10 %
motor-model error, all 4 actual runs failed; with the right voltage all 4 finished on the shortest
path. That is why the two-resistor divider is the first upgrade to make.
